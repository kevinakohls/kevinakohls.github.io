If It's a Behavior Problem, It's a Habit Problem.

Training transfers knowledge. Procedures describe what people should do. Metrics tell us what happened. Leadership can demand compliance.

But none of those things guarantees that tomorrow—when production falls behind, the supervisor changes, or the improvement champion leaves—people will continue doing what they were taught.

Under pressure, old habits compete with the new process. And the old habits have an enormous advantage: they have already been practiced and rewarded.

CUE → ACTION → REWARD → REPEAT

Habit Engineering deliberately designs the conditions that allow better behaviors to become easier and more natural to repeat.

The Habit Engineering Method

Don't try to change everything. Focus improvement where it matters, identify the behaviors necessary to achieve it, and engineer those behaviors into habits.
